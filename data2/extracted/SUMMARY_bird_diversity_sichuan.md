## C) SEASONAL / MIGRATION PATTERNS

### S3 (Chengdu) — Seasonal Patterns (Strongest Quantitative Data)

**Bird species count by season:**
- Winter (168 spp, 20,807 individuals) > Spring (156 spp, 16,794) > Autumn (143 spp, 14,858) > Summer (114 spp, 11,791)

**Winter is the peak season** — due to:
1. Large numbers of wintering waterbirds congregating at Minjiang, lakes, reservoirs, urban parks
2. Altitudinal migrants descending from mountains to lower elevations in winter

**Diversity indices all follow:** Winter > Spring > Autumn > Summer

**Chengdu as a migration corridor:**
- Located on key migratory routes (mountain-to-plain transition)
- 61 summer visitors + 60 winter visitors + 41 passage migrants = 162 migratory species (62% of Chengdu's total)

### S2 (Shaluli Mtns) — Migration
- Located on **Central Asian-Indian** AND **East Asian-Australasian** flyways
- Rich migratory bird diversity
- Surveyed in Aug, Oct (2019), Jan, May, Aug (2020), Jan, Apr (2021) — covering all seasons

### S1 (Xiayong NR) — Seasonal
- Surveys: April (migration/breeding) and September (post-breeding/migration)
- Located on migratory corridor but major waterbirds appear to bypass the reserve, choosing nearby Napa Lake (纳帕海) in Shangri-La instead

- 2,100–3,000 m: Arid valley shrub (干旱河谷灌丛)

**Key finding:** Water wetland has fewest species but HIGHEST individual count (waterbird congregations) — important for power line collision risk assessment!

**Shannon-Wiener Index Order:**
Subalpine coniferous > Alpine shrub meadow > Mixed forest > Arid valley shrub > Subice-snow shrub > Farmland > Water wetland

**Habitat Similarity (Jaccard):**
- Subalpine coniferous ↔ Alpine shrub meadow: IsJ = **0.50** (highest)
- Subalpine ↔ Mixed forest: 0.49
- Farmland ↔ Arid valley shrub: 0.48

**Elevation Pattern — MID-PEAK MODEL:**
- Species richness peaks at **2,800–3,600 m** (mid-elevation)
- Low at <2,000 m and >4,400 m
- This peak is higher than nearby areas (Gongga: 800–1,800 m; Meigu Dafengding: 2,600–3,000 m; Heizhugou: 1,600–2,400 m) — likely due to the overall higher average elevation of the study area
